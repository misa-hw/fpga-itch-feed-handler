Test: scripts/board_linerate.sh (scripts/fpga_connect.sh wraps it)
  tcpreplay sends build/tracked.pcap (1,655,589 frames, 1,752,946 MoldUDP64
  messages) to eno1 (Intel I219, e1000e) on a direct cable, 1000 Mb/s full
  duplex. tcpdump records the frames the kernel hands to the NIC; the full
  frames are rebuilt in that order (scripts/rebuild_sent.py) and the
  reference book (`pcapgen expect -mold-receiver`) is run on them with the
  hardware's MoldUDP64 rule. PASS requires the board's accepted frames,
  message/duplicate/gap counters, top-of-book change count and CRC-32 hash
  of every change to equal the model's on that stream, with no FCS errors,
  frame errors or overflows, and no tcpdump drops.

The sender does not send the pcap faithfully at speed. At the kernel's
transmit tap it drops frames and reorders others (interleaved runs about
1,900 frames apart), with the default fq_codel qdisc and with a FIFO; the
NIC has one transmit queue. tcpreplay counts those frames as sent.
  rate           frames sent            dropped    out of order
  20,000 pps     99,997 of 100,000      3          0
  60,000 pps     1,655,585 of all       4 (last)   0
  top speed      99,272 of 100,000      728        3,912
  top speed      1,655,466 of all       123        65,262
Running the hardware's MoldUDP64 rule over the captured order reproduces
the board's duplicate and gap counters exactly in every run, so the board
received exactly what was sent. (Before this was known, a comparison with
the unmodified pcap failed: 715,441 duplicates at top speed.)

  All constraints met: WNS +0.045 ns, WHS +0.038 ns; GMII inputs unchanged
